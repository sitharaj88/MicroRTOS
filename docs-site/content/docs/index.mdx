MicroRTOS is a real-time kernel for 8-bit AVR and ARM Cortex-M microcontrollers.
It's small enough to fit in 3.5 KB of flash on an ATmega328P, modern enough to
support EDF scheduling and tickless idle, and disciplined enough to align with
MISRA C:2012.

## What makes it different

- **Hardware-validated** — every release is flashed to an Uno and proven to
  blink with `rtos_task_delay`. Not just "compiles clean."
- **Pay-as-you-go features** — `RTOS_USE_*` flags gate every advanced module.
  Disabled features cost zero bytes; the linker strips them.
- **Single header** — `#include "rtos.h"` is the entire public surface.
- **Static allocation** — no `malloc`. Every TCB and stack is `static uint8_t`
  in your code, sized at compile time.
- **Two ports today** — AVR (ATmega328P, ATmega2560, ATmega32U4) and
  ARM Cortex-M (SAM3X8E, SAMD21). Adding a new port is ~500 lines.

## What it doesn't do

MicroRTOS is a kernel, not a full BSP. It does not include:

- Device drivers (UART, SPI, I²C, ADC) — you bring your own.
- A filesystem or network stack.
- Dynamic loading or multi-process isolation.

If you need those, look at Zephyr or NuttX. If you want a small, predictable
scheduler you can fully understand in an afternoon, you're in the right place.
